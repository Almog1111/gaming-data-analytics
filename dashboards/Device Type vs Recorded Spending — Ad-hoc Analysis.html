“Players (all)” counts every player in the group, including those with no recorded purchase amount. The average, median and interval use recorded amounts only; missing amounts are never replaced with zero. “Unknown” is a separate category for the 60 players with no device recorded; it is not a device type. “Small sample” marks groups with fewer than 30 recorded amounts.

Business summary (full dataset)

  • Overall: iOS players have the higher average recorded amount ($111.05 vs $94.67 for Android; +$16.38, about 17% higher). Players with no device recorded (“Unknown”) average $161.61, but only 56 have a recorded amount.
  • Not a clear difference: amounts are highly skewed (median $12.84 for iOS vs $11.57 for Android), the 95% intervals overlap, a Welch t-test gives p≈0.36, and a rank-based Mann–Whitney test is borderline (p≈0.05).
  • Segment mix is similar: Whales are 2.13% of Android and 2.20% of iOS players, so the overall gap does not come from a different mix. Within segments the direction is mixed: iOS is higher for Minnow (+$0.44) and Whale (+$370.42; 33 Android vs 26 iOS recorded players) but lower for Dolphin (−$9.60). None is statistically clear (p≈0.07 Minnow, 0.50 Dolphin, 0.28 Whale).
  • Unknown device: 60 players, 4 of them Whales (6.7%), which explains its high average; it is not a platform and should not be interpreted as one.

Business meaning: in this dataset, device type alone does not reliably distinguish average recorded spending. The apparent iOS advantage is small relative to the variability and rests mainly on a small number of Whale players. These are descriptive associations and do not show that device type affects spending.

Recommended next step: before making any platform-specific monetization or marketing decision, re-test the iOS–Android difference within the Whale segment (59 recorded Whales in total) using a larger sample or dated transaction-level data over a defined period.

Limitations

  • Segments (Minnow / Dolphin / Whale), the exact meaning of the purchase amount and the collection period are unverified in the source dataset.
  • Recorded amounts are highly skewed: a few Whale players account for most revenue, so averages (and their intervals) are unstable. Intervals shown are a normal approximation and indicative only.
  • Only device type is available (no OS version, model or acquisition channel). Results are descriptive associations and do not show that device type influences spending.
